# Part I — Theory

## 1. Fundamental Magnetic Theorems

### Biot–Savart Law
The magnetic field $\vec{B}$ generated by a steady current $I$.
$$d\vec{B} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi} \frac{d\vec{l} \times \hat{r}}{r^2}$$
* $\mu_0$: vacuum permeability, $4\pi \times 10^{-7}$ T·m/A
* $d\vec{l}$: vector length element of the wire
* $\hat{r}$: unit vector from the wire element to the field point
* $r$: distance from the wire element to the field point

### Ampère's Law
Relates the integrated field around a closed loop to the enclosed current.
Useful only for highly symmetric configurations.
$$\oint \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{l} = \mu_0 I_{enc}$$

### Faraday's Law of Induction
$$\mathcal{E} = -N \frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}$$

For **sinusoidal steady state** at angular frequency $\omega = 2\pi f$, this
becomes the form used everywhere in this project:
$$\boxed{\;|\mathcal{E}| = \omega\,\lambda = \omega N \Phi_B\;}$$
This is the identity `sanity_check.py` enforces on every FEMM row as
$V_{rx} = \omega \Phi_{rx}$ (FEMM reports *flux linkage*, so $N$ is already
included).

### Lenz's Law
The induced current opposes the change in flux that produced it — the physical
meaning of the minus sign above.

### Magnetic Energy
$$W = \tfrac{1}{2} L I^2 \qquad\text{and}\qquad W = \frac{1}{2\mu_0}\int B^2\,dV$$
Equating these is the *energy method* for extracting $L$, and is essentially
what a FEM solver does internally.

---

## 2. Magnetic Flux ($\Phi_B$) and Flux Linkage ($\lambda$)

$$\Phi_B = \iint \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{A}
\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad \Phi_B = B A \cos\theta \;\;\text{(uniform field)}$$

Distinguish carefully — this is the most common source of factor-of-$N$ errors:

| Symbol | Name | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\Phi_B$ | magnetic flux | through **one** turn | Wb |
| $\lambda = N\Phi_B$ | flux **linkage** | total linked by all $N$ turns | Wb·turns |

FEMM's `mo_getcircuitproperties` returns **flux linkage** $\lambda$, which is
why the code computes $M = \lambda_{rx}/I_{tx}$ with no extra $N$.

---

## 3. Self-Inductance ($L$)

**Definition:**
$$L = \frac{N \Phi_B}{I} = \frac{\lambda}{I} \qquad\text{and}\qquad \mathcal{E} = -L\frac{dI}{dt}$$

### 3.1 Ideal (long) solenoid
$$L = \frac{\mu_0 N^2 A}{l} \qquad \textbf{valid only if } l \gg R$$

### 3.2 Single circular loop (wire radius $a$, loop radius $R$)
$$L \approx \mu_0 R\left(\ln\frac{8R}{a} - 2\right) \qquad \textbf{valid only if } a \ll R$$
Generalised to $N$ turns concentrated in a thin bundle:
$$L \approx \mu_0 N^2 R\left(\ln\frac{8R}{a} - 2\right)$$

### 3.3 Short multi-turn coil (single layer)
$$L \approx \frac{\mu_0 \pi R^2 N^2}{l + 0.9R}$$

### 3.4 Wheeler's multilayer formula — **the one that applies here**
For a coil of mean radius $a$, winding length $b$, and winding depth $c$
(all in **inches**, result in µH):
$$L[\mu H] = \frac{0.8\,a^2 N^2}{6a + 9b + 10c}$$

Converting to SI ($1$ in $= 0.0254$ m): each length scales by $1/0.0254$, so the
numerator gains $(1/0.0254)^2$ and the denominator $(1/0.0254)$, leaving one net
factor:
$$L[\text{H}] = \frac{0.8}{0.0254}\times 10^{-6}\,\frac{a^2N^2}{6a+9b+10c}
= \boxed{\;\frac{31.50\times10^{-6}\,a^2 N^2}{6a + 9b + 10c}\;}\quad (a,b,c \text{ in m})$$

> **Note.** `sanity_check.py` uses `31.6e-6`. The exact conversion is
> $0.8/0.0254 = 31.496$. The code is **+0.33 %** high — negligible against
> Wheeler's own ~1–2 % accuracy, but worth knowing when reconciling digits.

Accuracy: ~1 % provided $b > 0.2a$ (satisfied here: $b/a = 0.71$).

---

## 4. Mutual Inductance ($M$)

**Definition and reciprocity:**
$$M = \frac{N_2 \Phi_{1\to2}}{I_1} = \frac{N_1 \Phi_{2\to1}}{I_2} = \frac{\lambda_2}{I_1}
\qquad M_{12} = M_{21}$$
$$\mathcal{E}_2 = -M\frac{dI_1}{dt} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad |V_2| = \omega M I_1 \;\;\text{(AC)}$$

### 4.1 Neumann formula (exact, general)
$$M = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\oint_1\oint_2 \frac{d\vec{l}_1 \cdot d\vec{l}_2}{|\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_2|}$$

### 4.2 Coupling coefficient
$$M = k\sqrt{L_1 L_2}, \qquad 0 \le k \le 1$$

### 4.3 Magnetic dipole field — the approximation used in this document

Far from a current loop, the coil looks like a magnetic dipole of moment
$$\vec{m} = N I A\,\hat{n} = N I \pi R^2\,\hat{n}$$

The dipole field has two limiting directions:

| Position | Field | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| **On axis**, distance $d$ | $B_{ax} = \dfrac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\dfrac{2m}{d^3} = \dfrac{\mu_0 m}{2\pi d^3}$ | parallel to $\vec m$ |
| **Equatorial** (broadside), distance $d$ | $B_{eq} = \dfrac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\dfrac{m}{d^3}$ | **anti**-parallel to $\vec m$ |

The equatorial field is exactly **half** the on-axis field and points the
opposite way.

### 4.4 Side-by-side coils with parallel (vertical) axes — **our geometry**

Two coils whose axes are both vertical and which are separated **horizontally**
by $d$ put each coil in the other's **equatorial** plane. Assuming the field is
uniform over the secondary and equal to its centroid value:

$$\Phi_{1\to2} = B_{eq}A_2 = \frac{\mu_0 N_1 I_1 R_1^2}{4d^3}\,\pi R_2^2$$
$$\lambda_2 = N_2\Phi_{1\to2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\boxed{\;M = \frac{\mu_0 \pi N_1 N_2 R_1^2 R_2^2}{4\,d^3}\;}$$

The $4$ in the denominator *is* the equatorial factor-of-two. This is exactly
`dipole_M_coplanar()` in `sanity_check.py`.

**Validity:** requires $d \gg R$. The error is quantified in Part IV.

### 4.5 Coaxial loops (for reference — *not* our geometry)
$$M \approx \frac{\mu_0 \pi N_1 N_2 R_1^2 R_2^2}{2(R_1^2 + d^2)^{3/2}}$$
Note the $2$ rather than $4$: on-axis field is twice the equatorial value.

---

## 5. Field of a Coil

**On the axis of a single loop**, distance $z$ from centre:
$$B_z = \frac{\mu_0 I R^2}{2(R^2+z^2)^{3/2}}$$

**Finite solenoid** (length $l$, radius $R$, $n = N/l$), on axis at $z$ from centre:
$$B_z = \frac{\mu_0 n I}{2}\left(\frac{l/2-z}{\sqrt{R^2+(l/2-z)^2}} + \frac{l/2+z}{\sqrt{R^2+(l/2+z)^2}}\right)$$

At the **centre** ($z=0$) this reduces to the form used in Part III:
$$B_{centre} = \mu_0 n I \frac{l/2}{\sqrt{R^2 + (l/2)^2}}$$
which tends to $\mu_0 n I$ only as $l/R \to \infty$.

---

## 6. AC Effects

**Skin depth:**
$$\delta = \sqrt{\frac{\rho}{\pi f \mu_0}}$$

**AC resistance** (round wire, $x = a/\delta$) — the piecewise approximation used
throughout this repo:
$$\frac{R_{AC}}{R_{DC}} = \begin{cases} 1 + \dfrac{x^4}{48}, & x < 1 \\[2ex] \dfrac{x}{2} + \dfrac{3}{4} + \dfrac{3}{32x}, & x \ge 1\end{cases}$$

**Quality factor and resonance:**
$$Q = \frac{\omega L}{R_{AC}}, \qquad C_{res} = \frac{1}{\omega^2 L}, \qquad V_{cap} = I\,\omega L = \frac{I}{\omega C}$$

# Part III — Worked Proof (follow along)

Coil: the Part 2.2 reference coil. Target to land on: **FEMM's axisymmetric
solve, $L = 3.180$ mH.**

## Step 1 — Turns from the winding window

The winding cross-section (the "window") is depth × length:
$$A_{win} = c \times b = 0.010 \times 0.025 = 2.500\times10^{-4}\ \text{m}^2$$

One wire occupies $\pi a_w^2$; only a fraction (packing) of the window is copper:
$$N = \frac{\text{packing}\times A_{win}}{\pi a_w^2}
= \frac{0.70 \times 2.500\times10^{-4}}{\pi (5.12\times10^{-4})^2}
= \frac{1.750\times10^{-4}}{8.2354\times10^{-7}}$$

$$\boxed{N = 212.49\ \text{turns}}$$

## Step 2 — Choose the right inductance formula

**This step is the whole proof.** Test each candidate's validity *before* using it:

| Formula | Requires | Actual | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ideal solenoid §3.1 | $l \gg R$ | $l/R = 0.025/0.035 = \mathbf{0.71}$ | **fails badly** |
| Single loop §3.2 | thin bundle, $a_w \ll R$ | bundle is $10\times25$ mm vs $R=35$ mm | **not thin** |
| Wheeler §3.4 | $b > 0.2a$ | $b/a = 0.71$ ✓ | **use this** |

The coil is *shorter than it is wide* and has a *fat* winding cross-section. It
is neither a long solenoid nor a filament.

Confirming the rejected formulas really are wrong:

$$L_{ideal} = \frac{\mu_0N^2\pi R^2}{l} = 8.735\ \text{mH} \quad (2.67\times \text{too high})$$
$$L_{loop} = \mu_0N^2R\!\left(\ln\tfrac{8R}{a_w}-2\right) = 8.548\ \text{mH}\quad (2.62\times\text{too high})$$

Both overestimate by more than 2.6×. **Picking the formula is the calculation.**

## Step 3 — Wheeler's formula

$$L = \frac{31.50\times10^{-6}\,a^2N^2}{6a+9b+10c}$$

Denominator:
$$6(0.035) + 9(0.025) + 10(0.010) = 0.210 + 0.225 + 0.100 = 0.535$$

Numerator:
$$31.50\times10^{-6}\times(0.035)^2\times(212.49)^2
= 31.50\times10^{-6}\times1.225\times10^{-3}\times4.5154\times10^{4}$$
$$= 1.7423\times10^{-3}$$

$$L = \frac{1.7423\times10^{-3}}{0.535} = 3.2564\times10^{-3}\ \text{H}$$

$$\boxed{L \approx 3.256\ \text{mH}}\qquad(\text{3.267 mH using the code's }31.6)$$

**FEMM solved 3.180 mH — agreement to 2.4 % (2.7 % with the code constant).**
That is well inside Wheeler's stated accuracy. *The formula is validated.*

## Step 4 — Magnetic field at the coil centre

Turn density:
$$n = N/l = 212.49/0.025 = 8499.8\ \text{turns/m}$$

Infinite-solenoid value (the naive answer):
$$\mu_0 n I = (1.2566\times10^{-6})(8499.8)(2.5) = 26.70\ \text{mT}$$

Correct finite-length value:
$$B_{centre} = \mu_0 n I\,\frac{l/2}{\sqrt{R^2+(l/2)^2}}
= 26.70\times10^{-3}\times\frac{0.0125}{\sqrt{0.035^2+0.0125^2}}$$
$$= 26.70\times10^{-3}\times\frac{0.0125}{0.037165} = 26.70\times10^{-3}\times0.33635$$

$$\boxed{B_{centre} = 8.981\ \text{mT}}$$

> The finite coil produces only **33.6 %** of the infinite-solenoid field — a
> direct consequence of $l/R = 0.71$, the same reason Step 2 rejected the ideal
> formula.

## Step 5 — Flux and flux linkage

Mean turn area:
$$A = \pi R^2 = \pi(0.035)^2 = 3.8485\times10^{-3}\ \text{m}^2$$

From the inductance (the definitional route):
$$\lambda = LI = 3.2671\times10^{-3}\times2.5 = 8.168\times10^{-3}\ \text{Wb·turns}$$
$$\Phi_{turn} = \lambda/N = 8.168\times10^{-3}/212.49 = 3.844\times10^{-5}\ \text{Wb}$$

$$\boxed{\Phi_{turn} = 38.44\ \mu\text{Wb},\qquad \lambda = 8.168\ \text{mWb·t}}$$

**Independent cross-check** via the field of Step 4:
$$\Phi \approx B_{centre}A = 8.981\times10^{-3}\times3.8485\times10^{-3} = 34.56\ \mu\text{Wb}$$

Ratio $38.44/34.56 = 1.11$ — the two routes agree to **11 %**. This is the
expected level: representing a $10\times25$ mm multilayer winding by a single
mean radius and one centre-field value cannot do better. Agreement at this level
confirms no gross error.

## Step 6 — Resistance and skin effect

Wire length:
$$l_w = N\,2\pi R = 212.49\times2\pi\times0.035 = 46.73\ \text{m}$$

DC resistance:
$$R_{DC} = \frac{\rho l_w}{\pi a_w^2} = \frac{1.68\times10^{-8}\times46.73}{8.2354\times10^{-7}} = 0.9533\ \Omega$$

Skin depth at 20 kHz:
$$\delta = \sqrt{\frac{1.68\times10^{-8}}{\pi\times20000\times4\pi\times10^{-7}}} = 0.4613\ \text{mm}$$

Since $x = a_w/\delta = 0.512/0.4613 = 1.110 \ge 1$, use the second branch:
$$\frac{R_{AC}}{R_{DC}} = \frac{1.110}{2} + 0.75 + \frac{3}{32(1.110)} = 0.555+0.75+0.0845 = 1.3894$$

$$\boxed{R_{AC} = 0.9533\times1.3894 = 1.3245\ \Omega}$$

> $\delta < a_w$, so current is already crowding into the surface — skin effect
> is **not** negligible here. This is why `sanity_check.py` asserts
> $\delta < a_w$ at the operating frequency.

## Step 7 — Q and resonance

$$Q = \frac{\omega L}{R_{AC}} = \frac{125663.7\times3.2671\times10^{-3}}{1.3245} = \boxed{310}$$

$$C_{res} = \frac{1}{\omega^2 L} = \frac{1}{(125663.7)^2\times3.2671\times10^{-3}} = \boxed{19.38\ \text{nF}}$$

$$V_{cap} = I\omega L = 2.5\times125663.7\times3.2671\times10^{-3} = \boxed{1026\ \text{V peak}}$$

> **The design consequence.** Driving 2.5 A through this coil at 20 kHz puts
> **over 1 kV** across the tuning capacitor. Resonance does not remove the high
> voltage — it *relocates* it from the supply onto the capacitor. This is
> precisely why `optimize_air_core.py` sizes designs against 250 / 630 / 1000 /
> 2000 V capacitor classes.

## Step 8 — Mutual inductance (vertical axes, side-by-side)

**Configuration:** both coil axes vertical, separated horizontally by $d$; the
secondary sits in the primary's **equatorial** plane. Field assumed uniform over
the secondary, equal to its centroid value.

Take $d = 120.837$ mm (the FEMM centre-to-centre separation) with
$N_1 = N_2 = 212.49$ and $R_1 = R_2 = 35$ mm.

**8a. Dipole moment**
$$m = N I A = 212.49\times2.5\times3.8485\times10^{-3} = 2.0444\ \text{A·m}^2$$

**8b. Equatorial field at the secondary's centroid**
$$B_{eq} = \frac{\mu_0 m}{4\pi d^3} = \frac{(4\pi\times10^{-7})(2.0444)}{4\pi(0.120837)^3}
= \frac{10^{-7}\times2.0444}{1.7644\times10^{-3}}$$
$$\boxed{B_{eq} = 115.87\ \mu\text{T}}$$

**8c. Flux through one secondary turn**
$$\Phi_{1\to2} = B_{eq}A = 115.87\times10^{-6}\times3.8485\times10^{-3} = 445.9\ \text{nWb}$$

**8d. Linkage and $M$**
$$\lambda_2 = N_2\Phi_{1\to2} = 212.49\times445.9\times10^{-9} = 94.76\ \mu\text{Wb·t}$$
$$M = \frac{\lambda_2}{I_1} = \frac{94.76\times10^{-6}}{2.5}$$

$$\boxed{M = 37.90\ \mu\text{H}}$$

**Verify against the closed form** of §4.4:
$$M = \frac{\mu_0\pi N_1N_2R_1^2R_2^2}{4d^3}
= \frac{(4\pi\times10^{-7})\pi(4.5154\times10^{4})(1.5006\times10^{-6})}{4(1.7644\times10^{-3})}
= 37.90\ \mu\text{H}\quad\checkmark$$

## Step 9 — Coupling coefficient

$$k = \frac{M}{\sqrt{L_1L_2}} = \frac{37.90\times10^{-6}}{3.2671\times10^{-3}} = \boxed{0.0116}$$

Only **1.2 %** coupling — expected for air-core coils separated by 3.45 radii.
$k \ll 1$ confirms the coils are weakly coupled, which is exactly the regime an
axle counter operates in: the *change* in this weak coupling when a wheel passes
is the signal.

## Step 10 — Induced signal voltage

$$|V_{rx}| = \omega M I_1 = 125663.7\times37.90\times10^{-6}\times2.5 = \boxed{11.9\ \text{V}}$$

(Open-circuit, before any resonant step-up.)

## Step 11 — $M$ versus separation

$M \propto 1/d^3$, so separation dominates everything:

| $d$ (mm) | $d/R$ | $M$ (µH) | $k$ | Dipole approx. quality |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 80 | 2.29 | 130.62 | 0.0400 | poor |
| 100 | 2.86 | 66.88 | 0.0205 | poor |
| **120.84** | **3.45** | **37.90** | **0.0116** | **marginal (FEMM spacing)** |
| 150 | 4.29 | 19.82 | 0.0061 | fair |
| 200 | 5.71 | 8.36 | 0.0026 | good |
| 250 | 7.14 | 4.28 | 0.0013 | good |
| 300 | 8.57 | 2.48 | 0.0008 | good |

Doubling the separation cuts $M$ by **8×**. Mounting distance is the single most
important mechanical tolerance in the installation.

# Part IV — Error Budget (read before trusting the numbers)

| # | Assumption | Status at our geometry | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Wheeler applies ($b>0.2a$) | holds, $b/a=0.71$ | ~1–2 % |
| 2 | Code's Wheeler constant 31.6 vs exact 31.496 | minor | +0.33 % |
| 3 | Dipole approximation ($d \gg R$) | weak, $d/R = 3.45$ only | **overestimates** |
| 4 | Uniform $B$ over the secondary | **poor** — see below | **overestimates** |
| 5 | Coils treated as circular filaments at $R_{mean}$ | real winding is $10\times25$ mm | ~10 % |
| 6 | No steel rail in the hand calculation | rail shields flux | **overestimates** |

### Quantifying assumption 4

The field falls as $1/d^3$, so across the secondary's own diameter
($\pm R = \pm35$ mm about $d = 120.84$ mm):

$$\frac{B(d-R)}{B(d)} = \left(\frac{120.84}{85.84}\right)^3 = 2.79
\qquad
\frac{B(d+R)}{B(d)} = \left(\frac{120.84}{155.84}\right)^3 = 0.47$$

The true field varies from **2.79×** down to **0.47×** the centroid value across
the secondary. Treating it as uniform is therefore a genuinely crude step at this
spacing — a deliberate simplification, not an exact result.

Because $1/d^3$ is convex, the near side gains more than the far side loses, so
the *centroid* value tends to **under**-represent the true average — while the
dipole approximation itself **over**-estimates at small $d/R$. The two errors act
in opposite directions. Treat $M = 37.9\ \mu$H as a **±30–50 % order-of-magnitude
figure**: reliable in its $1/d^3$ *scaling*, not in its absolute value.

> **Rule of thumb:** trust the hand calculation for *ratios and trends*
> ($M\propto N^2$, $M\propto 1/d^3$, $L\propto N^2$). Trust FEMM for *absolute*
> values, because only it includes the rail.

| $M_0$ (FEMM planar model) | — | 0.9269 µH | **different geometry — see §2.4** |

**What this proves.** Steps 1–7 reproduce the simulation's reference-coil values
exactly, and Wheeler's inductance lands within 2.4 % of an independent FEMM
axisymmetric solve. The solver's units, turn handling and material setup are
therefore trustworthy.

**What it does not prove.** The 8.394 µH dipole figure matches
`sanity_check.py`'s arithmetic exactly — but that is a *free-space upper bound*,
not a prediction of the rail-shielded planar model's 0.9269 µH. Those are
different physical problems, and the 9× gap is the flux the steel rail removes.

## Appendix B — Scaling laws worth memorising

$$L \propto N^2 \qquad M \propto N_1N_2 \qquad M \propto \frac{1}{d^3}
\qquad M \propto R^4 \;(\text{equal coils}) \qquad V \propto \omega M I$$

$M \propto N^2$ is **FEMM-verified** in this project (≤1.8 % deviation over
$N = 50\text{–}200$). The linear-in-area scaling used by the analytic
extrapolation scripts is **not** solver-verified — treat large area scale factors
as guidance only.
